# Optimization research for Trading-bot-Nobitex

Research date: 1 October 2026. This assessment uses the current working-tree code, including uncommitted independent RSI work, primary research papers, and official library documentation. It does not establish that any alternative beats this project's Differential Evolution (DE) in an experiment: no strategy optimizer benchmark was run. Recommendations below are project-specific engineering judgments to test.

The recommended next experiment is **multivariate TPE through Optuna versus the existing DE**, evaluated under the same chronological validation and cost assumptions. Retain DE until the comparison supports a change. Improving the evaluation protocol and risk constraints has higher priority than replacing the search algorithm.

**What the project actually uses.** `optimizer.py` searches integer parameters using SciPy DE: `best2bin`, Sobol initialization, population multiplier 20, mutation dithering `(0.5, 1.0)`, crossover 0.7, tolerance 0.01, and no polishing. AdvRSI has 11 searched parameters, TrendMomentum 10, and ExtremeReversal 12. Independent RSI runs separate 10-parameter searches for long and short with separate capital. Its removal of each side's unused opposite entry threshold already reduces the search space appropriately.

Default search ranges extend indicator lookbacks and smoothing to 100 candles. Independent RSI uses RSI/stochastic lengths and smoothing of 2–100, fast RSI of 2–50, RSI moving average of 2–40, lower bands of 1–35, upper bands of 65–99, and entry waits of 0–10. Combined RSI retains entry waits of 0–100. MACD's fast period is capped at 99 so its slow period can remain strictly larger within the 100-candle limit. Oscillator thresholds stay within 1–99, with separate lower and upper entry bands. The widest defaults require 400 warmup candles; the fetch reserves this history separately from evaluation candles. Broader ranges do not guarantee a global optimum and may slow convergence. Saved custom bounds remain in effect until reset.

The objective is deterministic for fixed data, parameters and execution settings. The search itself is stochastic; uncertainty about future market performance is separate. Integer threshold rules and changing entry/exit timing make the surface discontinuous, so the existing gradient-free global search is a reasonable baseline.

The implementation already has useful foundations: data fetched once, process-based parallel evaluation, stored seeds and inputs, fixed evaluation boundaries based on the largest allowed warmup, fees/slippage/holding-cost controls, next-open fills, reserved capital, and a chronological test that begins flat. These should remain part of any replacement. `docs/backtesting.md` describes the current accounting more accurately than older repository guidance.

**Local saved settings and results.** A read-only query of `.runtime/trader.sqlite3` found local optimizer preferences for ZECUSDT, AdvRSI, 4-hour candles, 2,100 evaluation candles, 10 USDT credit, 1x leverage, 16 workers, balanced scoring, broad RSI ranges, and 5,000 generations. This is the local runtime database; a running app using another data directory or per-user database may have different settings. `optimizer.json` is a legacy migration input, not authoritative evidence of the current selection.

Four saved local optimizer jobs include three cancelled searches and one error. None has a train/test split; their stored execution settings have zero fees, zero slippage and zero holding costs. The cancelled results include:

| Leverage | Closed trades | Win rate | Maximum drawdown | Total marked P&L | Closed-trade net P&L |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5x | 2 | 100% | 61.74% | 175.54 USDT | 97.96 USDT |
| 1x | 3 | 100% | 20.13% | 23.22 USDT | 8.55 USDT |
| 1x | 8 | 87.5% | 23.08% | 17.67 USDT | 17.67 USDT |

These are historical cancelled results, not completed experiments or evidence of current behavior. The current balanced objective requires at least 30 closed training trades by default and a selected minimum win rate, so these small-sample winners would now be rejected. Their recorded difference between total and closed P&L also demonstrates why open-position marks must be reviewed separately. The historical records lack the newer eligibility-floor fields.

At continuous 4-hour spacing, 2,100 candles cover about 350 days; a 70/30 split leaves about 245 training days and 105 test days. Actual calendar coverage depends on gaps. Large smoothing lengths can produce few trades even on this much data. Thirty trades are a useful filter, not proof of independent observations or a reliable edge.

**Computation is larger than the displayed estimate suggests.** Sobol initialization rounds DE's requested population up to a power of two. For the current 10–12 parameter strategies, `20 × dimensions` becomes 256 population members. At 30 generations, the full budget is 7,936 objective evaluations; at 5,000, it is 1,280,256 per search. Independent long and short searches can together reach 2,560,512. These are ceilings if every generation completes, before parent-process summary/chart work; convergence or cancellation can end a run earlier. [SciPy DE documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.differential_evolution.html).

The parallel progress estimate in `optimizer.py` uses the unrounded `20 × dimensions`, so AdvRSI progress counts understate this Sobol population. Use measured objective calls and unique backtests when comparing alternatives. Increasing generations finds better fits to the searched history; it does not add observations or establish future performance.

**Candidate search methods, ranked for this project.**

| Method | Why it merits consideration | Tradeoff | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Optuna multivariate TPE | Learns promising regions from prior trials; accepts integer variables and parameter interactions | Needs informative completed trials; parallel suggestions and sampler overhead can reduce its advantage | First challenger to DE |
| SMAC with a random-forest surrogate | Designed for algorithm configuration, including discrete/conditional spaces and evaluations across instances | Additional dependencies and surrogate overhead; verify Python 3.13/Windows support for the chosen release | Second challenger if TPE is inconclusive |
| Existing DE with explicit budgets and restarts | Already integrated; directly explores the discontinuous integer space and batches well across CPUs | Potentially many evaluations; repeated integer candidates waste work | Keep as the baseline |
| Multi-objective TPE or NSGA-II | Exposes return/drawdown tradeoffs rather than hiding them in one profit score | Produces a set of choices; needs a selection policy fixed before testing | Add if a Pareto view is useful |
| Random or Sobol sampling | Cheap, easily parallelized reference for whether adaptation helps | Does not learn from previous scores | Include a simple benchmark baseline |

TPE models parameter distributions associated with better and worse scores and proposes promising configurations. Its original research treats discrete and conditional configuration spaces, but its reported wins are machine-learning experiments, not evidence of trading performance here. [Original TPE paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/4443-algorithms-for-hyper-parameter-optimization.pdf).

For this project's interacting indicator periods, explicitly enable multivariate sampling. Group decomposition is useful if one study later contains conditional strategy branches; separate fixed-strategy studies do not need that extra machinery. Running-trial handling helps parallel suggestions avoid nearby unfinished configurations. Constraint APIs vary by Optuna release; pin the version and use its documented interface. [Official TPESampler documentation](https://optuna.readthedocs.io/en/stable/reference/samplers/generated/optuna.samplers.TPESampler.html).

Keep process workers for the present Python simulation loop. A parent-owned ask/tell scheduler can propose parameters, dispatch pure evaluations, persist summaries, and report outcomes to the study. This fits the existing architecture without having every worker write to the application's SQLite database. Durable study state should retain completed trials and permit continuation, while excluding incompatible data, bounds or execution settings. Optuna also documents shared-storage multiprocessing. [Optuna parallel execution](https://optuna.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorial/10_key_features/004_distributed.html).

SMAC is a credible technical alternative because its random-forest approach supports complex configuration spaces and tuning across problem instances. Historical development windows can serve as instances if the evaluation policy is defined carefully. It may outperform TPE on these rules, but that remains unmeasured. [SMAC3 paper](https://jmlr.org/papers/v23/21-0888.html), [official configuration and scenario guide](https://automl.github.io/SMAC3/latest/3_getting_started/).

Multi-objective optimization can maximize cost-adjusted return while minimizing equity drawdown. NSGA-II is one option; Optuna also supports multi-objective TPE. It is not necessary to adopt another genetic algorithm just to get multiple objectives. Keep minimum evidence and liquidation rules as constraints, then show the feasible tradeoffs. [Official NSGA-II documentation](https://optuna.readthedocs.io/en/stable/reference/samplers/generated/optuna.samplers.NSGAIISampler.html).

Random search is a useful baseline because it can beat grids when only some dimensions matter. That result comes from hyperparameter studies and must be checked on these strategies. Sobol is an additional space-filling baseline, not the same algorithm as random search. [Random-search research](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v13/bergstra12a.html).

**Improve what gets optimized first.** Current balanced scoring maximizes uncapped marked net P&L after eligibility floors. It does not penalize or limit drawdown, require positive returns, or check stability across periods. Win-rate-only scoring can reward a single closed winner. Large-move scoring counts absolute moves, including losing trades, so it is unsuitable as the main profitability objective.

Use a constraint-first return objective. Keep the user's win-rate floor if desired, add a predeclared maximum equity drawdown and a liquidation policy, and require enough completed trades across development history. Consider both marked net return and closed-trade evidence. Risk limits must reflect the intended margin strategy and capital allocation; the spot manager's 10% limit should not silently become a universal margin-trading rule.

A practical development objective is median net return across equal-duration chronological windows, subject to risk and evidence constraints. Report the worst window, dispersion, closed trades, exposure and cost-stressed results alongside it. This is a proposed heuristic, not a published universal optimum. Avoid dozens of adjustable reward weights that themselves become another tuning exercise. When no candidate qualifies, report no eligible result.

The current balanced score transforms eligible P&L using `-(2/pi) × atan(P&L/credit)`. It preserves the ranking but compresses large positive profits toward -1. SciPy stops according to population-score spread, so this transformation can affect termination. For example, P&L/credit ratios of 50 and 100 map to approximately -0.98727 and -0.99363; a population consisting equally of those scores has relative spread 0.00321, below tolerance 0.01, despite a twofold profit difference. This calculation demonstrates a possible convergence effect, not a diagnosis of a saved run. Revisit stopping with a budget and a minimum meaningful improvement in raw development returns, keeping feasibility handling explicit.

**Chronological validation matters more than optimizer branding.** Ordinary Optimizer searches all evaluation candles. Optimizer Tester correctly reserves one later block, but repeated runs chosen after observing that block turn it into development data. Searching many configurations creates selection bias; the backtest-overfitting literature specifically cautions that a simple holdout alone can be inadequate. [Probability of backtest overfitting](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4w1110bb).

For fixed parameters, reserve a final chronological period before searching. Tune and select using several earlier development windows, then freeze the method, parameter set, costs and risk policy before opening the final period. Any window repeatedly scored during optimization is part of development, even if called validation. Nested forward evaluation provides a stronger assessment: at each outer boundary, choose parameters using only earlier data and apply them to the next block.

If the intended bot will periodically retune, test that complete deployment schedule. At every simulated retuning date, train on trailing or expanding history ending at that date, freeze the chosen parameters, and evaluate the following interval. Choosing one parameter set using later windows and then claiming its earlier simulated returns were out of sample would be invalid. Scikit-learn provides ordered expanding splits and a gap, but application-specific boundary rules remain necessary. [TimeSeriesSplit documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.TimeSeriesSplit.html).

Preserve causal indicator warmup. For these rule strategies, the current flat start prevents training positions from crossing into held-out evaluation; a blanket gap equal to the maximum indicator period is unnecessary solely because indicators use historical candles. For PriceAI, AgenticAI and spot forecast labels, exclude labels whose future outcome reaches the next boundary. The spot engine already does label purging and two chronological validation folds plus an untouched final test; build on that instead of replacing it with the RSI optimizer's simpler split.

Search breadth across symbols, timeframes, strategies and seeds is also part of the experiment count. Store trial summaries and time-indexed equity returns. Deflated Sharpe Ratio can then help account for selection and non-normal returns, but requires appropriate return data and an estimate of effective independent trials. Raw optimizer call count is not that estimate. PBO/CSCV is a further diagnostic, not a substitute for causal forward deployment evaluation. [Deflated Sharpe Ratio paper](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf).

**Execution and parameter robustness.** Explicitly configure account costs and adverse fills. Use predeclared cost stress on development windows, then confirm it after selection. The optimizer currently fetches Binance candles by default, whereas live execution is Nobitex; compare overlapping venue data and validate finalists on Nobitex where sufficient reliable history exists. Better search cannot remove price, spread, liquidity or fill differences between venues.

For finalists, perturb periods and thresholds within their valid bounds and replay the same development protocol. Prefer settings whose neighbors remain acceptable over an isolated high-profit spike. Allocate a fixed local-check budget; testing the entire neighborhood grows exponentially. Check interactions before freezing apparently unimportant parameters. For independent RSI, retain separate directional searches but evaluate the combined time-aligned equity, drawdown and exchange capacity; combined drawdown cannot be obtained by summing two maximum drawdowns.

Cache duplicate integer configurations after repair, keyed by data snapshot, strategy, parameters, evaluation boundary, capital, leverage, execution settings and model version. A bounded per-worker indicator cache may help when period combinations recur. At present `_Decisions` constructs a new `Indicators` instance on every research evaluation, so indicator memoization does not span optimizer trials. Profile before changing the simulator; a faster but behaviorally different execution model invalidates comparisons.

**Methods to defer.** Plain gradient descent and L-BFGS are poor first choices for threshold-based integer rules. Continuous CMA-ES or Gaussian-process BO can be adapted to discrete inputs, but require justification against DE/TPE/SMAC here. Another generic genetic algorithm or particle swarm method has no demonstrated advantage in this repository. Reinforcement learning changes the policy-development problem and is not a direct replacement for tuning the existing RSI rules.

Hyperband/ASHA deserves special caution. The published method saves resources through staged allocation and early stopping. A short backtest prefix can represent a different market regime, rather than a faithful cheaper measurement of full-period performance. Pruning a swing strategy after an early losing period may discard the later winner. First measure rank agreement between cheap and complete development evaluations; use hard feasibility rejection or conservative screening before profit-based prefix pruning. [Hyperband paper](https://jmlr.org/papers/v18/16-558.html).

**A concrete benchmark before choosing a default.**

1. Freeze data snapshots, bounds, costs, risk constraints, starting equity, warmup and chronological boundaries. Begin with the saved ZECUSDT/4-hour configuration and add at least one other liquid market and a materially different historical regime, subject to data availability. Keep a fresh final period sealed.
2. Compare DE, multivariate TPE and Sobol sampling first; add SMAC if the initial comparison warrants its dependencies. Use at least three predeclared search seeds on development data and identical resource limits. Seed equality does not imply identical randomness across algorithms.
3. Compare both elapsed-time budgets and objective-call budgets. With the current 256-member DE population, seven generations plus initialization consume up to 2,048 calls; use that as an initial ceiling for each competitor. Record completed calls, unique simulations, duplicates and overhead. Early-converged DE runs may use fewer calls; do not pretend the budgets were equal in that case.
4. Count the actual work: a candidate scored on three windows under two cost scenarios requires six simulations. Separate candidate suggestions from window/scenario evaluations. Give neighborhood checks the same reserved budget for every method.
5. Select the algorithm and settings using inner development data and nested forward results. Compare feasible net return, drawdown, worst-window behavior, cost sensitivity, neighbor stability and time to a useful candidate. For inference about this particular strategy, historical research performance is more relevant than a generic optimizer leaderboard.
6. Freeze the complete winner-selection procedure before the final period. Evaluate the selected procedure once, preserve its result, and follow with paper operation. If the final result fails, record failure; subsequent tuning needs new untouched evidence.

The implementation order is evaluation/risk controls and accurate counters, then duplicate caching and profiling, then a TPE challenger, then the controlled comparison. For spot ML, the corresponding extension is TPE tuning of a modest HGB/LightGBM parameter space within the existing purged chronological folds. Do not use the already inspected September spot test as an untouched target for new tuning.

The research supports adding and measuring alternatives. It does not support promising higher live profits, a fixed speedup, or an optimizer that consistently dominates DE on this project without running the comparison above.
